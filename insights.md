# Business Insights — Duka Smart Electronics

All figures come from `queries.sql` and follow the README business rules: money = **Completed orders only**,
revenue = `quantity * unit_price * (1 - discount / 100)`, and "today" = **30 September 2026**, the latest
order date in the data. Amounts are in KSh.

**Headline numbers:** 800 orders from 138 customers; 634 Completed orders bringing in **24,155,371.70** in
revenue and **2,890,921.70** in profit, an overall margin of about **12%** (Q13, Q33).

---

## 1. Accessories sell the least but earn the best margin

**Finding.** Accessories make only **4.46%** of revenue (KSh 1,077,496.10), the smallest category (Q16, Q42).
But their profit margin is **42.39%**, against 9.9%–11.4% for every other category. As a result they earn
KSh 456,746.10 in profit, almost the same as Home Appliances (KSh 458,034.05) on a quarter of the revenue (Q33).

**Why it matters.** Big-ticket phones, laptops and TVs drive revenue but keep only about 10 shillings of
every 100 as profit. Each shilling of accessory revenue earns about four times as much profit as a shilling
of anything else.

**Recommendation.** Sell accessories with every phone, laptop and TV: chargers, earbuds and mice at the
till, plus bundle offers such as "phone + charger + earbuds". A small rise in accessory sales improves
profit more than the same rise in any other category.

---

## 2. Discounts are wiping out profit — 62 orders were sold below cost

**Finding.** Completed orders received **KSh 1,522,246.30** in discounts. That is equal to **52.7%** of the
profit that was left. **62 Completed orders** were discounted below cost, losing **KSh 141,978.70**
outright. The worst products are the Hotpoint Refrigerator (16 loss-making orders, KSh 38,716.50 lost),
iPhone 13 (8 orders, KSh 29,008.40) and Hisense 43in TV (13 orders, KSh 13,213.20). (Q33; analysis of
revenue vs cost per order.)

**Why it matters.** The big-ticket items have list margins of only 9%–15%. A Samsung 55in TV
(margin 9.0%) or a Hotpoint fridge (9.5%) loses money at any discount of 10% or more, yet discounts of
10%–25% are routinely given on them.

**Recommendation.** Set a maximum discount per product that stays above cost, e.g. no more than 5% on TVs
and fridges, and require manager approval for anything higher. Move promotions to the high-margin
accessories instead.

---

## 3. A small group of customers brings in most of the money

**Finding.** Using value bands (Q45), **28 High Value customers** (KSh 300,000+ each, about a fifth of
buyers) bring in **63.75%** of revenue (KSh 15.4M). The 73 Low Value customers (under KSh 100,000)
bring in only 13.35%. The top five customers alone — Brian Chebet, Peter Ndungu, Lucy Njoroge, Nancy Korir
and Purity Ochieng — spent between KSh 698,921.60 and 848,118.75 each (Q24, Q46).

**Why it matters.** Losing even a few of these customers would remove a large share of revenue. Notably,
most of the top spenders are in the **Retail** segment, not VIP: the company's own VIP label does not match
who actually spends the most.

**Recommendation.** Start a loyalty programme for High Value customers (named account contact, early access
to new phones, free delivery). Review the VIP segment using actual spending, so the label goes to the
customers who earn it.

---

## 4. A third of buying customers have gone quiet

**Finding.** **47 of the 132 buying customers** (36%) have not completed an order in more than 90 days
(Q34). They include **6 of the 19 VIPs** and 3 High Value customers. Together these 47 customers spent
KSh 4.6M in the past. The longest gap is Agnes Kimani, at 625 days. A further **12 registered customers have
never ordered at all** (Q23).

**Why it matters.** Winning back an existing customer is usually cheaper than finding a new one, and these
customers have already shown they will spend.

**Recommendation.** Run a win-back campaign over M-Pesa/SMS, starting with the lapsed VIP and High Value
customers. Offer them an accessory voucher, which brings them back on the highest-margin category. Send
the 12 never-ordered customers a first-purchase offer.

## 5. Sales are growing fast, but January collapses after December

**Finding.** Revenue for January–September 2026 was **KSh 12.83M**, against **KSh 6.89M** for the same
months of 2025: growth of about **86%** (Q38, Q43). The three best months are all recent: August 2026
(KSh 2.24M), September 2026 (2.09M) and June 2026 (2.04M) (Q31). But December 2025 (KSh 2.03M) was followed
by **January 2026 at KSh 322,432.95**, the lowest month in the data: a drop of **84%** (Q40, Q46).

**Why it matters.** The business is growing strongly, and demand peaks in December. The January slump
means cash flow and staff are under pressure right after the busiest month. The strong months also
expose the business to the discount problem in Insight 2: more sales at a loss.

**Recommendation.** Plan stock and staff for a December peak. Use a January "back-to-school / new-year"
campaign focused on laptops, printers and accessories to soften the dip. Don't use deep discounts to chase
January sales, because of the thin margins in Insight 2.

---

## 6. Nairobi is 40% of revenue; other towns are under-served

**Finding.** Nairobi customers produced **KSh 9,581,980.35**, about **40%** of all revenue, from 41 buying
customers (Q25). Mombasa is a distant second at KSh 3.16M. Machakos has only 3 buying customers
(KSh 745,099.90), and Nyeri has 7 (KSh 994,102.20).

**Why it matters.** Heavy dependence on one city is a risk, and it shows how little of the other nine towns
the company has reached.

**Recommendation.** Keep Nairobi stock levels high. Test targeted promotions or a delivery partner in the
smaller towns (Machakos, Nyeri, Eldoret, Kisumu), where even a few extra customers would make a visible
difference.

---

## 7. Pending orders are worth KSh 3.18M — and 11% of orders get cancelled

**Finding.** **76 Pending orders** are worth **KSh 3,178,652.40**, about 13% of Completed revenue. **90 orders
(11.25%) were cancelled** (Q20), worth KSh 3,465,355.60.

**Why it matters.** Pending orders are money already agreed but not yet collected, and the oldest go back
to February 2025. Cancelled orders are lost sales.

**Recommendation.** Follow up pending orders weekly and close or cancel anything older than 30 days.
M-Pesa is used for 55% of orders, so send M-Pesa payment prompts to make it easy to pay. Record a cancellation
reason on every cancelled order, so the next analysis can find out why customers cancel.
